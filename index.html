  • Glenoid Implant: CAD & Finite Element Modeling

    Timeline
    Sept. 2026 – Jan. 2027
    Context
    Academic project, IBHGC
    Format
    Team (6)
    Tools
    CATIA V5, Abaqus, Octave

    Objective

    Understand why the glenoid component of a shoulder prosthesis loosens over time, and design an implant that reduces this risk. The project is run with the Institute of Human Biomechanics Georges Charpak (IBHGC) and supervised by Dr. Nathalie Maurel and Dr. Amadou Diop.

    Approach

    • Reviewed the literature on glenoid loosening as a team.
    • Modeled the classic keeled implant and its bone substitute in CATIA V5.
    • Built a finite element model of this implant in Abaqus, based on a compression test at 150 N.
    • Measured shoulder kinematics with electromagnetic sensors and computed joint angles from transformation matrices in Octave.
    • Ran a loosening test on the cemented implant: 1 mm of PMMA cement, load applied at 22° and 5 mm from the edge, relative displacement tracked by camera.

    My role

    I built the CATIA V5 model of the implant and its bone substitute, and its Abaqus model, on my own. The compression and loosening tests were run by the team.

    Results

    • No loosening at 750 N or 900 N, under static and cyclic loading.
    • Loosening began at 1,400 N, the baseline our new design has to beat.
    • Next steps: design a new implant, combining a keel and a peg, that resists loosening better than this baseline (project ends January 2027).

  • Nicomatic: Production Internship on the Shop Floor

    Context
    Industrial internship
    Format
    Individual
    Tools

    Approach

    Results

    • Through deburring and sorting, I brought molded parts to specification.
    • I suggested improvements to work order documentation and step-by-step instructions for new operators.

  • Aluminum Casting, Machining & Design of Experiments

    Timeline
    March – June 2026
    Context
    Academic project
    Format
    Team (5)
    Tools
    Design-Expert, CNC lathe, profilometer

    Objective

    Find which material and process factors govern the surface roughness of a machined aluminum-silicon part, from casting to the finished surface.

    Approach

    • Sand-cast 2 AlSi22 + Sr parts with fast cooling, with a fluidity spiral test and thermocouple tracking during solidification.
    • Turned both parts on a CNC lathe with a dynamometer, over 36 cutting conditions, and mapped the specific cutting energy (minimum near Vc = 560 m/min, f = 0.5 mm/rev).
    • Measured roughness (Rdq) with a stylus profilometer.
    • Pooled the data of the whole class (12 teams) into 5-factor, 2-level designs of experiments in Design-Expert.

    My role

    I took part in casting, machining and metrology with my team, and ran the statistical analysis with 1 teammate.

    Results

    • Keeping every replicate made all 3 models predictive (predicted R² from 0.49 to 0.63).
    • Silicon content governed roughness, ahead of strontium and cooling rate; machining parameters played a secondary role.

  • Tidal Turbine: Preliminary Design for a Coastal Town

    Timeline
    Feb. – May 2026
    Context
    Academic project
    Format
    Team (5)
    Tools
    Fusion 360

    Objective

    Propose a renewable power system that uses local marine resources to supply a coastal or island town of 50,000 people, with a 10-year service life and the lowest possible cost, then define its architecture and pre-size it. Supervised by Jean-Marc Périnet, Professor of Mechanical Design at Arts et Métiers.

    Approach

    • Studied the product's environment over its whole life cycle: currents, salt corrosion, biofouling, marine life, regulations, IEC TS 62600 standards and patents.
    • Benchmarked offshore wind, wave, tidal and floating solar solutions, then used weighted decision matrices to select a horizontal-axis tidal turbine, a gravity tripod foundation and 316L stainless steel.
    • Sized the rotor with Betz's actuator disk model (power coefficient 0.42, 90% drivetrain efficiency) for 500 kW at a nominal current of 3.5 m/s in the English Channel.
    • Traced the load path from the blades to the seabed and checked the blade root against the rotor thrust.

    My role

    I wrote most of the functional specification and of the study of the marine environment, standards and patents, and I was a main contributor to the benchmark of existing solutions and to the decision matrices. I carried out the rotor sizing with Betz's model together with 1 teammate.

    Results

    • A 9 m rotor (8.76 m required, rounded up), which delivers about 530 kW at 3.5 m/s.
    • Power scales with the cube of current speed: at 1.5 m/s the same rotor gives only 42 kW, so 13 machines would be needed to keep 500 kW at that speed.
    • A nominal thrust of 355 kN per machine, the load case for the mast and foundation, and a blade root stress of about 15 MPa, well below the 133 MPa allowed with a safety factor of 3.

  • Two-Stage Gear Reducer: Preliminary Design & 3D CAD

    Timeline
    Sept. 2025 – May 2026
    Context
    Academic project
    Format
    Team (5)
    Tools
    3DEXPERIENCE

    Objective

    Design a gear reducer for Arts et Métiers, acting as the client, from a functional specification to a complete 3D digital mock-up. Supervised by Jean-Marc Périnet, Professor of Mechanical Design at Arts et Métiers.

    Approach

    • Defined the architecture from the specification and a FAST diagram, and chose a straddle-mounted design over an overhung one for better load transfer.
    • Sized the gear pair against fatigue and pitting, and the shafts under combined torsion and bending (Tresca criterion), selecting 30CrMoNi8 steel.
    • Selected SKF bearings for a 16,000-hour service life and Paulstra seals, sized the keys, and checked that splash lubrication was sufficient.
    • Modeled and assembled the reducer in 3DEXPERIENCE.

    My role

    I led the preliminary design phase. In the CAD phase, I worked in a team of 2 on the variant without spacers and personally designed and assembled the upper shaft and the outer housing.

    Results

    • A 60-page technical report whose design meets the specification with margin on admissible power.
    • A fully assembled 3D model of the reducer.

  • Mixer Bearing: From Casting to Metrology

    Timeline
    Dec. 2025 – Jan. 2026
    Context
    Academic project
    Format
    Team (4)
    Tools
    Sand casting, manual lathe, 5-axis center, CMM

    Objective

    Manufacture a mixer bearing, from the drawing to the inspected part, to understand how each manufacturing step affects whether a part meets its functional requirements before it goes into production.

    Approach

    • Studied the part drawing and prepared the molding study: parting line, machining allowances, sprue, riser and core.
    • Made the mold in a sand of silica, clay and graphite, set the core, and cast the aluminum-silicon part.
    • Wrote the operation sheets (order of operations, tools, and locating of the part by long centering and an axial stop), then turned the first functional surfaces on a conventional lathe.
    • Added an intermediate soft-jaw operation to finish the second bearing bore, so that tool deflection would not affect the coaxiality of the 2 bearing seats.
    • Machined the remaining surfaces on a 5-axis machining center with a 32-tool magazine.
    • Inspected flatness, perpendicularity and 3 positions on a coordinate measuring machine (CMM), probing 7 points per plane and per cylinder.

    My role

    We carried out every step as a team. I took part in making the sand mold, pouring the metal, machining the part on the manual lathe and on the 5-axis machining center, and inspecting it on the CMM, where I probed points on the part.

    Results

    • A finished part, taken from sand casting through machining to CMM inspection.
    • Linked the CMM results back to the casting step, the main lever for final accuracy.

  • Hygroscopic Hydrogels for Atmospheric Water Harvesting

    Timeline
    March 2024 – July 2025
    Context
    Independent research (TIPE)
    Format
    Individual
    Tools
    Fusion 360, Python

    Objective

    Increase the amount of salt loaded into a hydrogel to maximize the water it can capture from humid air, a passive alternative to energy-intensive desalination, inspired by the droughts on my aunts’ farm in Taiwan.

    Approach

    • Started from Graeber, Díaz-Marín et al. (Advanced Materials, 2024) and corresponded for 10 months with its co-author, Carlos Díaz-Marín, who had just completed his PhD at MIT.
    • Loaded a commercial polyacrylamide hydrogel with salt.
    • Designed a water harvester in Fusion 360 and built prototypes from cut plastic bottles.
    • Measured swelling over 5 days in 5 salt concentrations, and solved a simplified swelling equation in Python with finite differences.

    My role

    I ran the whole project, from sourcing materials to experiments and modeling.

    Results

    • 1 g of dry gel absorbed up to 0.90 g of water overnight.
    • Swelling peaked at a salt concentration of 50% of the solubility limit.
    • My model predicted which samples would swell the most.
    • Grade: 19.6/20 at the national exam (A+ equivalent).
